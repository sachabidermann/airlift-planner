Method

Full method and sources

1. Airfield usability

USGS ShakeMap gives shaking intensity (MMI) on a grid, with values up to X. The intensity at each airport is mapped to a probability that it can take relief flights in the first days. The curve is a judgment call, set by hand while looking at the international backtests.

2. Need

USGS PAGER counts people at each intensity. Where PAGER was not run, exposure is rebuilt from US Census files. People at MMI VIII and above are the priority. Water is excluded; it is rarely flown at scale.

3. Airbridge

Heavy jets fly into a gateway airport. C-130s shuttle cargo on to airfields inside the damage zone. Throughput follows Air Force Pamphlet 10-1403. Cargo landing within 50 km of the damage center counts in full; the share falls to zero at the forward radius.

Limitations

  • A planning model, not an operational tool. Capacity is an upper bound: parking spots are assumed from airport size, not taken from real ramp plans.
  • The usability curve is judgment, and the international backtests use the same earthquakes it was set on.
  • Straight-line distances. No liquefaction, tsunami, fuel, customs, handling, weather, airspace or roads.
  • The airport table and Census population are current, including for replays of older events.
  • ShakeMap is revised repeatedly, sometimes years later. The version used is shown above.
